Signal Alternative Without a Phone Number: Honest Look

2026-10-21 · MurMur team

Signal is a good messenger. Its encryption protocol has been studied for years, the code is open, and the nonprofit behind it has a long track record. But registration requires a phone number — and that's exactly what some people can't or won't provide. If you searched for "Signal alternative without phone number", here is a plain comparison of four options: Signal, WhatsApp, Session and MurMur. Weak points included.

Why the phone number matters (and when it doesn't)

A phone number in Signal is not a privacy disaster on its own: the server stores very little and hides sender metadata. But your number is your identifier. Anyone who knows it can find your profile, and to create an account you need a working SIM — which in some countries means registration tied to your real identity. For most people this is a non-issue. For others it's the dealbreaker that starts the search. Both reactions are reasonable.

What the table can't show: maturity

"E2EE by default" and "no phone number" are not the whole story. A messenger is also its implementation quality, how long it has survived real attacks, and who checked the code.

  • Signal has been audited repeatedly and runs on its own well-studied protocol. It's the safest default for most people.
  • WhatsApp uses Signal's protocol, but the client is controlled by Meta, and metadata feeds a company whose business is advertising. Encryption protects message content, not who talks to whom.
  • Session has no phone number at all and routes traffic through an onion network. Open source, maintained by a foundation.
  • MurMur is a young project. The code is open (github.com/Oleg2796996/murmur) and the relay is a small Rust service in Europe that never sees plaintext — but there has been no independent security audit yet, the review community is small, and a PWA depends on the browser platform you run it in. We'd rather say this plainly than pretend it isn't so.

Who MurMur is a good fit for

  • You want an account with neither a phone nor an email — npub keys instead.
  • You can't or won't install store apps. MurMur is a PWA: open the site, add it to your home screen, push notifications work.
  • You need calls, not just text.
  • You prefer to read the code yourself rather than trust marketing: it's on GitHub.

When Signal is still the right choice

  • You want a protocol and clients that have survived years of audits and real-world attacks.
  • A phone number isn't a problem for you.
  • Your contacts are already there — messenger value is mostly network effect.
  • For sensitive work, maturity beats novelty.

FAQ

Is a messenger without a phone number less secure?

No. Security depends on the protocol and its implementation, not on the identifier. If anything, an account that isn't tied to your SIM is a privacy plus. The real caveat is maturity: newer projects have fewer independent eyes on the code, and MurMur hasn't had an external audit yet.

What is an npub key?

In MurMur your account is a key pair (npub), not a phone number. No phone, no email — registration is generating keys.

Does Session require a phone number?

No. Session generates a random Session ID that acts as your address.

Can I use MurMur without installing an app?

Yes. It's a PWA: it runs in the browser and can be added to the home screen. Push notifications are supported. It does depend on your browser and OS, which is a trade-off of the PWA approach.

Why does WhatsApp have E2EE but still collect data?

End-to-end encryption protects message content. Metadata — who contacts whom, when, how often — is separate, and that's what feeds Meta's systems.
