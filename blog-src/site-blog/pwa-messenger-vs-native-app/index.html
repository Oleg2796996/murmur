MurMur is a PWA messenger: what that means in practice

2026-10-21 · MurMur team

What a PWA actually is

A PWA (Progressive Web App) is a website that behaves like an installed application. You open murmurchat.org in your browser, choose "Add to Home Screen" — and the messenger gets its own icon, opens full-screen without browser bars, and keeps working after you close the tab. Under the hood it is still web technology (HTML, JavaScript, a service worker), but from the user's point of view the difference from a native app is smaller than most people expect.

MurMur is built this way on purpose. You install it on an iPhone or an Android phone without App Store or Google Play, without an account tied to your phone number or email — your identity is a cryptographic key (an npub), generated on your device.

What a PWA can do today

The stereotype that "web apps are toy apps" is about ten years out of date. Modern browsers expose most of the APIs a messenger needs:

  • Push notifications. On both Android and iOS, MurMur can wake you up when a message arrives, even with the app closed. On iOS this became possible in 2023, when Safari added Web Push support.
  • Camera and microphone. Voice and video calls work through the standard WebRTC interfaces. Encrypted calls in MurMur go through the same channels as in any native messenger.
  • Offline behavior. A service worker caches the app shell, so the messenger opens even with a poor connection. Messages you send while offline are queued and delivered later.
  • Background sync and clipboard, file sharing, contacts picker — support varies by platform, but the basics are there.

What a PWA cannot do — honestly

There are real limitations, and we would rather list them than pretend they don't exist:

  • Everything depends on the browser. A PWA runs inside the browser engine of your device. If the browser breaks, the app breaks with it. You cannot fix this on our side.
  • iOS is the weakest platform. Safari's Web Push needs the app added to the home screen; notifications are coarser than native ones; some web APIs are still missing or throttled. Apple has been improving this yearly, but Android remains the more complete PWA platform.
  • Some system integrations are limited. Deep OS-level hooks — custom widgets, tight Siri or Google Assistant integration, system-wide share targets in every context — are either absent or partial.
  • Storage can be stricter. Browsers can, in rare cases (long periods of non-use, low disk space), clear website data. For a messenger whose account is your cryptographic key, we recommend backing up your key regardless — losing it means losing the account, with no SMS-based recovery, by design.

Why no app store is a feature, not a bug

The absence of MurMur from App Store and Google Play is often read as a sign of an unfinished product. It is closer to the opposite — a deliberate trade with concrete benefits:

  • No gatekeeper in the middle. App stores can reject, delay, or remove apps according to their own policies. A PWA is published at a URL, and what ships is what we built. For a messenger whose main promise is that nobody can read your messages, not depending on a platform's approval is consistent with the idea.
  • Updates are instant. You reload the page and you are on the new version. No review queue, no waiting days for an update to propagate, no forced upgrade screens.
  • Smaller footprint. There is no hundreds-of-megabytes download; the app installs in seconds and takes a fraction of the space.
  • Same app on every device. One codebase runs on Android, iOS, and desktop browsers alike.

The honest cost side: store listing also brings discoverability and a baseline of trust that "a website" does not automatically have. MurMur is a young project with a small audience and no independent security audit yet, so we do not claim that skipping the stores makes it more trustworthy — the code is open source, and that is where scrutiny should come from, not from a review queue.

FAQ

Do I need to install anything from an app store?

No. Open murmurchat.org in your browser and add it to the home screen. That is the whole installation process.

Do push notifications really work on iPhone?

Yes, as long as the PWA is added to the home screen (this is a Safari requirement, not ours). Notification formatting and reliability are somewhat behind Android.

Can I make voice and video calls?

Yes. Calls are end-to-end encrypted and use the browser's camera and microphone, which you grant per site.

What happens if I clear my browser data?

You may lose the locally stored key, and with it access to your account — there is no SMS or email recovery. Back up your key. This is a property of the key-based identity model, not of PWAs specifically.

Why should I trust a messenger that isn't in the stores?

You shouldn't take our word for it in any case. The relay and client code is open source; the relay is built so it cannot read plaintext and stores encrypted messages only for a limited time. MurMur is young, unaudited, and small — use it accordingly, and read the code if you can.

Try it: open murmurchat.org, add it to your home screen, and see whether a PWA covers what you need.
